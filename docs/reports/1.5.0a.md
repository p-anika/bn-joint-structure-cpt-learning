## Errors found in the Phase 1 report

1. **Repo name**: it is `p-anika/bn-joint-structure-cpt-learning`, not `bayesian-network-learning`.
2. **Figure 4 BIC**: confirmed by Jirka (Sep 28, 2026) to come from 10-fold cross-validation.
3. **CPT split**: 10/30/4 was caused by a tie-breaking bug. With the tie rule, the existing
   scores give 16/24/4, confirmed by the re-run in step 1.5.1.
4. **The unfinished sentence**: for 2 parents, STD has 18 parameters and MLR 10.
5. **"STD can only tie MLR, never beat it"** is wrong for 2+ parents. STD always fits at least
   as well but pays a bigger penalty, so it can win when parents interact strongly. That just
   didn't happen in this data.
6. **"0 or 1 parent"** → all 10 STD winners have exactly 1 parent.
7. **The "Discrepancies" list**: points to items 2 and 3, and adds Jirka's confirmation that
   the paper's structure came from bnlearn hc.

## Results

Re-reading `results/cpt-type-summary.csv` with the tie rule from CLAUDE.md:

| | STD | MLR | OLR |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 as reported (tie bug) | 10 | 30 | 4 |
| Same scores, tie rule applied | **16** | **24** | **4** |
| Expected by the research plan | 16 | 24 | 4 |
| Paper (Table 2, hc structure) | 19 | 22 | 3 |

The six nodes that change are a13, a15, d6, d11, d12 and d13 (MLR → STD). In total 18 nodes
have STD and MLR within 1e-6: all 17 one-parent nodes plus d11. The largest gap among them is
4.5e-13. Two of the 18, d7 and d10, stay OLR because OLR genuinely beats both.

**How the split was checked** (logged in `docs/experiment-log.md`):

```python
import pandas as pd
d = pd.read_csv("results/cpt-type-summary.csv")
def pick(r):                                    # the CLAUDE.md tie rule
    s = {"STD": r.bic_std, "OLR": r.bic_olr, "MLR": r.bic_mlr}   # order = tie preference
    best = max(s.values())
    return next(t for t in s if s[t] >= best - 1e-6)
d["fixed"] = d.apply(pick, axis=1)
print(d.fixed.value_counts())                   # STD 16, MLR 24, OLR 4
```
